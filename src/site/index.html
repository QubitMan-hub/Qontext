Tiles you touch collapse

Albers proved color depends on its neighbours. Quantum physics proves value does too.

In 1963 Josef Albers made one color look like two by changing what surrounded it. That is perception. Qontext goes further: here a color genuinely is different depending on its context, and the mathematics says no fixed coloring could ever match it.

Janus

Nine tiles, each a quantum observable. Read a tile with its row and it takes one color; read it with its column and it can take another. Both readings obey their rules. No fixed palette can.

What it's for

  • Brand systems that change with context (a page, a season, a partner) and stay mathematically coherent.
  • Interface states where one element really is different in each place it appears.
  • Posters and patterns that change as people look along rows or down columns.

Mermin's Dare

Give each tile a fixed value, +1 or −1, and try to satisfy all six rules. Five is the most anyone gets. A computer checked all 512 options; quantum measurement gets six, every time.

What it's for

  • A two-minute way to feel why a Qontext palette cannot be faked.
  • Onboarding for designers who have never heard of contextuality.
  • A classroom demonstration of quantum physics with nothing to install.

Unity Wheel

A system with d quantum levels has d phases spaced evenly around a circle. On the color wheel those are the classic harmonies: two levels make a complementary pair, three a triad, four a square.

What it's for

  • Perfectly balanced palettes from a single number.
  • Rotating a whole harmony at once, so brand colors can drift over time without falling out of balance.
  • Scroll through this room and the phase turns with you.

Fringe

Each color becomes a wave: hue is its phase, chroma its amplitude. Two colors meet at a beam splitter and leave through two ports. Shift the phase and the ports trade color while the total stays exactly the same.

What it's for

  • Paired gradients for light and dark modes that always balance each other.
  • Blends that cancel to gray in one place and double up in another.
  • Drag across the band to move the phase.

Pentacle

Five quantum events on a star where linked events exclude each other. Classical models cap their total chance at 2. One qutrit reaches the square root of 5, and the palette follows that geometry.

What it's for

  • Five-category palettes for charts and maps where neighbours must never look alike.
  • Star layouts and badges with color rules built in.

Spooky Pair

A light asset and a dark asset share an entangled pair. Each picks a style, and its accent is the measurement outcome. Their accents match and clash more reliably than any two independent rules could.

What it's for

  • Light and dark themes that stay linked without anyone matching them by hand.
  • Co-branded pairs that always answer each other.
  • Press measure and both cards change at once, however far apart they sit.

Unpaintables

Each shape is a set of directions in space. A classical coloring must light exactly one direction in every perpendicular set, and never two perpendicular ones. For Peres 33 and Cabello 18 that is impossible, so one set always breaks, and it glows.

What it's for

  • Generative art and 3D prints that carry a mathematical certificate of impossibility.
  • Star tilings and sculptures, exported from the Atelier as SVG and OBJ.

Notary

Run the circuits on IBM Quantum with Probe and load the results. Notary scores them against classical limits and seals the shots with a SHA-256 hash, the device name and the job record.

What it's for

  • Numbered editions with real provenance, and no blockchain.
  • Showing a client that a design came from a quantum computer, with the numbers to prove it.

This seal is sealed from the example pool that ships with Qontext, measured on a noisy simulator, and it says so. Seals from IBM hardware name the device.

Observer

Observer is a 12 KB script. It gives every visitor their own collapse, keeps it for their session, and exposes the colors as CSS variables. This page is running on it right now.

What it's for

  • Websites where every visitor gets a unique color system that is still on brand.
  • No server, no tracking, no network calls.
